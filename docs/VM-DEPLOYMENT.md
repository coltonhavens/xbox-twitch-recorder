## Shutdown and runtime defense

1. `recorder.supervisor` launches the worker in its own process group, with a **5h30 maximum**. Success/error exits emit redacted terminal events; timeout/termination kills the whole child process group (TERM, then KILL). An actual subprocess timeout was tested locally, without shutting down any host.
2. `xtr-archive.service` has **RuntimeMaxSec=5h45m**, `KillMode=control-group`, no automatic restart, and privileged `ExecStopPost=systemctl --no-block poweroff`. Normal success, no-work, disabled mode, worker error and supervisor timeout all lead to guest shutdown. Forcing service stop is a host power-off operation.
3. Independent guest `xtr-poweroff.timer` powers off at **5h50 after boot** even if the archive service is stuck. `xtr-boot-deadline.timer` checks at **five minutes** and powers off if the archive service never became active. This covers setup/environment/secret-loading failures. Neither relies on a successful worker import or alert API.
4. Native Compute Engine **maxRunDuration=21600 seconds**, **instanceTerminationAction=STOP**, automaticRestart=false supplies the independent six-hour platform stop. Require this verified setting before first enable; it works even if the guest is broken. The seven-hour state lease exceeds this limit. See [official VM time-limit documentation](https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/instances/limit-vm-runtime).

Guest shutdown must be verified to leave the VM TERMINATED and release ephemeral IPv4. Do not use suspension or a reserved static IP. Persistent boot disk and pending GCS objects remain billable after stop. Hard limits preserve state and already validated media; partial download can need a repeat. Do not raise a runtime limit without re-reviewing all outer limits, lease, ledger and budget model.

## Cost safeguards actually implemented in source

All budget/state mutations occur under the existing generation-conditioned global GCS lease. Missing new-month budget initializes normally; malformed existing counters fail closed. Deleting budget/state records can defeat accounting and deduplication, so never clear them to bypass a pause.

- Reserve **six hours** against a **120-hour UTC monthly work allowance** before scanning/downloading. Normal completion reconciles elapsed wall time plus two minutes overhead (minimum one minute). Killed/unknown runs keep their full reservation: safe overcounting instead of forgetting failed work. Crossing month-end can shift at most one six-hour execution; cost planning includes that margin. Once a six-hour reservation no longer fits, work pauses and alerts. Scheduler can still boot a VM that promptly exits; the budget model allows startup overhead.
- Reserve **40 GiB** before each new full download against a **250 GiB monthly staged-byte allowance**, then reconcile actual successful bytes. Failed/killed attempts keep the reservation. This counts repeat downloads, not just completed files. There is no bitrate downgrade or silent split.
- Stop each output at **40 GiB** and require room for that reservation within **100 GiB live staging**. Check all live staging objects, including orphan objects, not just journaled ones. A cap can pause even a smaller file that would have fitted: intentionally conservative. Buckets must be dedicated to this worker and media soft delete/versioning disabled for the planned storage bound to represent billed retained media.
- At most **five expensive attempts per VOD**, with waits of 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 hours. Individual request/fragment retries are bounded as before. A cap or exhausted budget pauses processing for operator review; it can delay later VODs, so respond to alerts. Remote-file reconciliation is allowed even after the final ambiguous attempt so a successful upload can still be recognized without another upload or download.
- Detect unfinished staging older than **24 hours**, alert without deleting it. Preserve the archive, Drive IDs and metadata on failure. No automatic destructive cleanup or budget reset. Completed-media cleanup remains checksum-confirmed and idempotent.

These are **resource allowances, not an invoice meter or guaranteed billing cap**. They cannot stop charges from unrelated project resources, changed prices/settings, external bucket writers, prolonged infrastructure failures, or repeated boots that fail before the ledger is available. Platform budget notifications are delayed and do not enforce a $10 spending cap. No automatic billing-account disablement is implemented.
